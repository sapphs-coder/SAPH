# SAPH — Creator Creative Direction

**Status:** Initial working profile  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-04  
**Purpose:** Capture confirmed creative preferences without inventing the creator's personality or treating one reference as a universal template.

## Current direction

SAPH should help create content that feels personal, natural and engaging, with polish where it serves the story. Creative choices should adapt to the content rather than force every video into one formula.

## Voice-over direction

**Preference: a mixture, selected to suit the content.**

Possible modes:
- Chatty and spontaneous — relaxed, friend-like delivery.
- Polished and confident — clear, composed narration that still feels personal.
- Funny and opinionated — humour, perspective and honest commentary.

These are working modes, not a claim that the creator always speaks in these ways. SAPH should learn the creator's actual vocabulary, rhythm, humour, opinions and boundaries from approved examples and direct feedback.

### Voice-over rules
- Match the narration to the content, mood and audience.
- Avoid generic influencer language and forced enthusiasm.
- Do not invent personal experiences, opinions or product claims.
- Preserve natural phrasing; do not over-script every moment.
- For brand work, remain truthful and recognisably personal.

## Transitions and editing direction

**Preference: a mixture, selected to suit the footage and story.**

Transition families to consider:
- Smooth — seamless cuts and flowing movement.
- Creative — gesture-led transitions, camera movement and match cuts.
- Trend-led — quicker social-video cuts where they genuinely fit.
- Cinematic — deliberate, polished visual changes where appropriate.

### Editing rules
- Choose transitions based on footage, movement, rhythm, music and story.
- Do not add effects just to make an edit look busy.
- Keep transitions technically achievable with the available footage.
- Preserve continuity and the creator's natural presence.
- Use faster pacing selectively; not every moment needs a cut.

## Initial content context

This direction was first calibrated using a beauty / get-ready-with-me reference. It may inform beauty, fashion and everyday lifestyle content, but must not automatically be applied to travel, food, fitness, reviews or other formats without checking fit.

## Learning and approval

For every new preference:
1. Record what was observed separately from what the creator explicitly liked.
2. Ask for feedback when the preference is uncertain.
3. Convert confirmed feedback into a reusable rule.
4. Keep the source reference and the resulting rule linked.
5. Treat creator approval as the gate before a pattern becomes a default.

## Still to learn

- Exact vocabulary, phrases and expressions the creator naturally uses.
- Humour style and topics or language to avoid.
- Preferred voice-over energy, pace and level of scripting.
- Music and sound preferences.
- Colour, lighting, framing and camera movement preferences.
- Transition examples the creator particularly likes or dislikes.
- Platform-specific preferences for TikTok and Instagram.

Do not fill these gaps with assumptions.
