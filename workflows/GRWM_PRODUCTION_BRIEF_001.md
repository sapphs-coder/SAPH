# SAPH Production Brief 001 — Natural GRWM with Personality-Led Voice-over

**Status:** First draft for creator review  
**Reference:** TikTok GRWM reference recorded in `visual/REFERENCE_LIBRARY.md`  
**Platforms:** TikTok and Instagram Reels  
**Format:** Vertical 9:16  
**Purpose:** Create a natural beauty / get-ready-with-me video with stronger narration and intentional transitions.

> This is a reusable first-pass brief, not a final script. Product names, personal anecdotes, opinions, soundtrack and exact footage must come from the creator.

## Creative intention

A personal, relaxed get-ready-with-me that feels like inviting the viewer into the routine. The video should have a clear progression and satisfying final look, while the voice-over supplies personality rather than describing every visible action.

## Voice-over direction

Use a flexible blend, selected moment by moment:
- Chatty and spontaneous for opening and routine moments.
- Polished and confident when explaining a choice or sharing a useful detail.
- Funny and opinionated when there is a genuine personal reaction.

### Draft narration structure

Use the prompts below to create an authentic script. Replace every bracketed prompt with the creator's real details; never invent them.

**Opening hook (0–5 sec)**  
“Come get ready with me — [the real reason, occasion or mood for getting ready].”

Alternative hook styles:
- Chatty: “Right, I'm getting ready and I thought I'd bring you along.”
- Opinionated: “[A real, specific thought or honest observation about today's look/routine].”
- Story-led: “I've got [real occasion/plan] coming up, so this is the look I'm going for.”

**Routine context (5–20 sec)**  
“I’m starting with [actual first step/product]. I’ve been [genuine experience or opinion, if supplied].”

**Middle section (20–70 sec)**  
Add one or two short personal observations, a useful detail or a light joke. Let the footage breathe; narration does not need to fill every second.

**Finishing touches (70–95 sec)**  
“Now for [actual final beauty/hair step]. I wanted the overall look to feel [creator's chosen description].”

**Reveal (final section)**  
“And this is the finished look.”  
Optional: add a genuine reaction or invite the viewer to share an opinion.

## Shot plan

| Beat | Footage | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | Mirror-facing opening or strongest finished-look teaser | Establish mood and give a reason to watch |
| 2. Setup | Medium mirror shot, getting products ready | Ground the viewer in the routine |
| 3. Details | Product held near camera; close-up of texture or application | Add visual variety and useful detail |
| 4. Routine | Makeup / skincare / hair clips, according to actual footage | Show progression without documenting every second |
| 5. Change-up | A gesture, turn, hand pass or camera movement | Motivate a transition into the next stage |
| 6. Finishing touches | Close details and final adjustments | Build anticipation |
| 7. Reveal | Full look, outfit and a natural pose or movement | Deliver the payoff |

## Transition plan

Choose transitions only when the footage contains the movement needed to make them convincing.

| Transition family | Suggested use | Footage cue |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth cut | Move between routine steps | Cut on a hand movement or action completion |
| Gesture match | Move from one stage to another | Hand passes lens, product crosses frame, hair flip |
| Match cut | Connect similar framing across stages | Same mirror position or body position before/after |
| Trend-led cut | Add energy to a short sequence | Beat hit or clear movement in the music |
| Cinematic reveal | Introduce the finished look | Turn, step into frame, lens wipe or deliberate camera movement |

Do not force a transition if the raw footage does not support it. Use a clean cut rather than fabricate continuity.

## Pacing and sound

- Open with the strongest visual or a clear spoken hook.
- Vary medium mirror shots with detail shots.
- Keep routine sections concise but not frantic.
- Leave short pauses where the visuals communicate clearly.
- Use music as support; narration must remain intelligible.
- Select soundtrack only after the creator approves the mood and platform-appropriate audio.
- No soundtrack, lyrics or exact audio choice has been approved yet.

## On-screen text

Keep text minimal and useful:
- Optional opening title: “GRWM” plus the real occasion, if there is one.
- Product labels only when accurate and useful.
- Optional short chapter labels for a longer routine.
- Avoid repeating every voice-over sentence as on-screen text.

## Edit checklist

- [ ] The first seconds establish a reason to keep watching.
- [ ] Voice-over sounds like the creator, not generic influencer copy.
- [ ] All personal claims and product opinions are genuine and approved.
- [ ] Shot order has a clear beginning, progression and payoff.
- [ ] Transitions are motivated by real movement or rhythm.
- [ ] Pacing varies without becoming chaotic.
- [ ] Music supports rather than competes with narration.
- [ ] Final reveal feels earned and complete.
- [ ] Export is vertical 9:16 and checked on a phone screen.

## Creator review

After the first edit, ask:
1. Which voice-over lines sound most like you?
2. Which lines feel unnatural or too scripted?
3. Which transitions should become recurring favourites?
4. Where does the edit feel too slow or too busy?
5. What should SAPH remember for the next GRWM?

Only creator-approved preferences should be promoted into permanent creative rules.
